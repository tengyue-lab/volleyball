# ELO System Audit and Simulation — Before Launch

I am preparing this volleyball app for public launch and want to improve its ELO rating system.

Before implementing any changes, please inspect the existing code, evaluate the current ELO system and run isolated simulations to recommend the best configuration.

**IMPORTANT: This is an investigation and testing task only. Do not change the production ELO algorithm, player ratings, database records, rank thresholds or team-balancing logic yet. Do not deploy.**

## 1. Context and goals

This is a social volleyball app where players are assigned to balanced teams and receive ELO updates based on completed match results.

The app has seven competitive tiers:

1. Iron
2. Bronze
3. Silver
4. Gold
5. Platinum
6. Diamond
7. Masters

For launch, I intend to start with a fresh player database and empty match history. All new players should begin at the lowest tier, Iron.

My goals are:

- Every new player starts at the same ELO baseline, within Iron.
- New players can move toward their appropriate skill rating relatively quickly.
- Established players have more stable ratings.
- Stronger and weaker players can gradually separate into meaningful skill tiers.
- Rating changes reflect match difficulty and expected results.
- Ratings do not fluctuate excessively from one Saturday to the next.
- The system remains fair for a small social volleyball community.
- The rating system works sensibly alongside the existing team-balancing algorithm.

I do not want to artificially force players into a bell-curve distribution or predetermined rank percentages.

## 4. Run isolated simulations

- Beginners.

Run enough simulated sessions to evaluate rating behavior over approximately 10, 30, 50 and 100 matches per active player where feasible.

## 5. Evaluate rating accuracy and distribution

For each candidate configuration, measure:

- ELO distribution after different numbers of matches.
- Minimum, median and maximum ELO.
- Rating spread and standard deviation.
- Number of players in each rank tier.
- How quickly stronger players move above weaker players.
- Whether players with different skill levels become meaningfully separated.
- How much ratings fluctuate after establishment.
- Whether ratings become stuck around the starting value.
- Whether ratings drift upward or downward across the whole population.
- Whether newer players gain an unfair advantage over established players.
- Whether players with low attendance are unfairly ranked.
- Whether the team balancer prevents ELO from identifying skill differences.

Where possible, compare the final rating order against the simulation's underlying player skill order.

Do not judge a configuration as successful merely because it produces a wider rating distribution. Rating accuracy and stability matter more than visual spread.

## 6. Investigate team-based ELO limitations

Because this is a team sport, please specifically investigate:

- Whether balanced teams result in most players maintaining approximately 50% win rates.
- Whether strong players can be underrated because they are consistently assigned weaker teammates.
- Whether weaker players can be overrated because they are consistently paired with stronger teammates.
- Whether individual provisional K-factors cause rating inflation or deflation.
- Whether the rating system remains fair when provisional and established players participate in the same match.
- Whether repeated teammates or opponents create rating bias.

Explain any limitations that cannot be solved using match wins and losses alone.

Do not change the team balancer during this investigation.
